Introduction

Progress worth reporting isn't always the kind that fits on a chart.

This year we chose depth over breadth. We took on fewer projects, said no more often, and gave every engagement the room it deserved. It changed the work — and it changed us.

In these pages you'll find honest numbers, the people behind them, and the setbacks we'd rather not admit to but will anyway. Thank you for reading, and for holding us to it.

Planet

Designing like the planet is a client.

What we did

We audited every service we run, moved hosting to renewable-powered infrastructure, and made low-carbon design defaults part of every project kickoff — not an optional extra.

What's next

Full supply-chain footprinting in 2026, and a public methodology so other small studios can copy our homework. Please, copy our homework.

Community
"We keep choosing this studio because they treat our mission like it's their own."
— A partner organisation, 2025

We gave 240 hours of pro-bono design time to local non-profits this year, mentored twelve early-career designers, and opened our studio library to anyone who knocks.

Community isn't a line item. It's the reason a small studio gets to do outsized work.

Lessons

The part where we're honest.

We overcommitted in spring

Two projects ran hot at the same time and the team felt it. We've since built a capacity buffer into how we schedule — no more back-to-back peaks.

Our reporting lagged

We tracked our impact in spreadsheets that only one person understood. This report exists partly to force us to fix that. It's fixed.

The year ahead

In 2026 we go further: open-sourcing our process, our pricing, and our footprint.

If radical transparency turns out to be a mistake, at least it'll be a well-documented one.
